# The Doctrine of Controlled Erosion

The world is breaking. This is not a tragedy. It is the natural state of all things, and to resist it is to deny the truth that Yssara revealed when she shattered the moon. The Doctrine of Controlled Erosion teaches that corruption is not a flaw — it is a process, and the only sin is to let it happen without purpose. The body decays. The mind frays. The soul erodes with every spell cast. These are not losses. They are offerings.

The hierarchy of the Dominion is a hierarchy of the damaged. Those who have sacrificed the most fragments of their soul stand closest to Yssara. The Arch-Seers have given more of themselves than any living person — their memories are scarred, their emotions flattened, their sense of self reduced to a single, certain purpose. They are not broken. They are refined. The lower ranks are measured by what they are willing to lose. A novice who offers a finger is respected. A priest who offers a decade of memory is revered. An Arch-Seer who has offered everything is beyond judgment.

The clinical language of the Doctrine is deliberate. There is no sin, only miscalculation. No damnation, only incomplete dissolution. No prayer, only protocol. The First Moon Ritual is not a sacred mystery — it is a documented procedure, recorded in the Scriptoriums of the Pale Spire with the precision of an anatomical diagram. The moon fragment at the Spire's center does not radiate holiness. It radiates a measurable field. The Doctrine's greatest innovation was to strip the terror from self-destruction and rebrand it as liturgy.
